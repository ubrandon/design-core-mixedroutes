Color rules

Clay is scarce. One accent moment per view. A full clay band is the loudest the brand gets, earn it with a statement.
Clay shouts, pine settles. Clay marks the invitation and the ask; pine marks the resolved state: you're in, it's locked.
Errors never wear clay. Any nearby red would read as the accent, so errors use brick and plain words.
No blues, teals, or neon. The only gradients allowed are photo scrims. The app's current teal predates this identity.

Type

Go where life is.

Display: Inter 800, tight tracking, short sentences that end with a period.

Body: DM Sans, regular, roomy line height. Warm and plain, it reads like a person, not a product tour.

Eyebrow labels   Inter 700, uppercase, wide tracking, clay.

Wordmark & type rules

MixedRoutes

The wordmark is the logo: Inter 800, ink on paper or white on photography. No swooshes, no pins, no gradient marks. Headlines are short sentences with a period, set heavy; no thin weights anywhere, and all-caps only for small eyebrow labels.

Imagery · the identity itself

Real light, mid-moment, unposed
Small groups, 2 to 8, the scale of a plan
Never: stock-photo smiles at the camera, phones as heroes, festival crowds, cold or moody edits, or screenshots floating in device mockups on white. Phone screens live inside sand or blush wells, cropped off the edge.

The signature: plan card

A 3:4 photo, 12px corners, warm scrim from the bottom, white Inter 800 title, one meta line, and the frosted going pill. Same anatomy everywhere: landing, social, ads, invites, merch. Vary the photo and the words, never the shape. The pill is the tell, keep its style exact.

Dark mode is a place, not an inversion

Warm darks, paper text.

Night surfaces come from roast and photo-scrim tones, text is paper rather than pure white, and clay and pine stay as they are. Never cool gray, never pure black, never a mechanical flip.

Three tests for any new surface: does a photo of real life carry the moment, does it feel warm and physical rather than cold and digital, and would it still feel like us printed on a shirt.
